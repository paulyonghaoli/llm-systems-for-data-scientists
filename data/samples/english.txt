The reviewer asked a fair question about the experiment: how many runs were
needed before the difference between the two configurations could be called
real rather than noise? The honest answer was that nobody had checked. The
first configuration had been measured once, the second had been measured once,
and the gap between them had been reported as a finding. When the runs were
repeated with different random seeds the gap moved around considerably, and on
four of the twenty seeds it pointed the other way entirely.

This is an ordinary failure and it is worth being unembarrassed about it. A
single measurement of a stochastic system is a sample, not a result. The
correction is not clever statistics; it is more runs, a stated interval, and a
willingness to say that the effect could not be distinguished from zero at the
sample size available. That last sentence is much more useful to a reader than
a number presented without one.
