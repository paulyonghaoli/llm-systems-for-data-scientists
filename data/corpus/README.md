# The retrieval corpus

Modules 3 and 4 and Capstone I all bind to this corpus. It is generated by
[`tools/build_corpus.py`](../../tools/build_corpus.py) from seed `20260811`,
measured by [`tools/verify_corpus.py`](../../tools/verify_corpus.py) (gate 25),
and that measurement is itself tested by
[`tools/test_verify_corpus.py`](../../tools/test_verify_corpus.py) (gate 26).
Real embeddings are recorded by
[`experiments/record_embeddings.py`](../../experiments/record_embeddings.py)
and checked by [`tools/verify_embeddings.py`](../../tools/verify_embeddings.py)
(gate 27).

```bash
python tools/build_corpus.py && python tools/verify_corpus.py
```

## What this is, and what it is not

**The prose is template-generated, not hand-written.** 2,419 documents at ~377
tokens each is roughly 0.91 M tokens; nobody authored that by hand and this
document is not going to imply otherwise. What was authored is the *generator
and its templates* — the seven document types, the twelve policy topics and
their rules, the register differences between them, and the phenomena planted
into them. The sentences themselves are recombinations.

That bounds what any result measured here is worth: **the corpus rewards
retrievers that handle the difficulties planted in it and says nothing about
difficulties nobody thought to plant.** A retriever that scores well here has
been shown to handle seven specific things, because those seven are measured
below. That is the entire claim.

Policies and FAQs are deliberately the *shortest* types despite being the most
important. They carry roughly a hundred and sixty words of topic-specific
content each, and the filler pool is shared across every topic, so a generous
filler draw buried the content that distinguishes them — see below.

**Documents, not chunks.** An earlier draft shipped pre-chunked text, which
left Module 3's chunking lesson with nothing to chunk. Gold labels are at
document granularity, as is standard in IR.

## The seven phenomena

Each is *measured*, not asserted. Thresholds come from what each phenomenon
*means*, not from what this corpus happens to produce.

| phenomenon | n | measured property | threshold |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `vocabulary_mismatch` | 30 | 97% have gold outside BM25 top-5; query-term coverage 0.26 | ≥50%; ≤0.55 |
| `lexical_distractor` | 31 | 94% have a non-gold BM25 top-1 | ≥50% |
| `near_duplicate` | 30 | 100% have a sibling at Jaccard ≥ 0.70 | 100% |
| `superseded` | 30 | 100% have a similar, non-gold predecessor | 100% |
| `multi_hop` | 31 | 100% need both documents for term coverage | ≥90% |
| `contradiction` | 24 | 100% have a rival document stating different numbers | ≥90% |
| `unanswerable` | 24 | mean top-1 score 12.4 vs 23.9 for answerable | strictly lower |
| `topic_distinctness` | 19 | a policy's own revision (0.68) beats any other topic (0.53) | revision closer |

Gate 25 uses a plain BM25 with untuned Robertson defaults (k₁ = 1.5, b = 0.75),
written out in `verify_corpus.py` rather than imported, so these numbers depend
on nothing outside this repository — and a tuned retriever would make "BM25
fails here" a statement about the tuning rather than about the corpus.

### Why `topic_distinctness` exists

It is the only check here that is not about a query, and it was added after
every other check passed on a corpus where **all twelve policies shared one
body and differed only in their title**. Nothing noticed. "BM25 does not find
the gold document" is satisfied just as well by twelve interchangeable
documents as by a genuine register gap, and "the gold document has a
near-identical sibling" was satisfied by the wrong sibling. Every check was a
*necessary* condition standing in for a sufficient one.

The FAQs had the same defect and worse: cross-topic Jaccard averaged 0.72 and
reached **1.00** — byte-identical documents on different subjects.

The invariant that pins it down: a policy's own superseded revision must be
more like it than any policy on a different subject. It is measured over
*distinctive* vocabulary, dropping terms that more than half the documents of
that type share, because plain Jaccard on a short document is dominated by
function words and read 0.74 between unrelated topics on "the", "you" and
"days".

## Embeddings

`BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` (MIT), pinned to commit `5c38ec7c` rather than `main`,
384 dimensions, driven through onnxruntime so no torch install is needed.
Shipped int8 with per-vector scales; cosine fidelity against float32 is
0.99984. Document, query and chunk vectors, at four chunkings (128, 256,
256/64-overlap, and 510 — the model has 512 positions and `[CLS]`/`[SEP]` take
two).

**Pooling was chosen by measurement and the first measurement was wrong.** The
model card specifies CLS; most code defaults to mean. Both come from the same
forward pass, so all four pooling × query-prefix combinations were scored on
the 200-query benchmark. On the *broken* corpus mean pooling won 10–0 with an
exact McNemar p = 0.0020, which looked decisive. On the corrected corpus the
same comparison is 12–5, p = 0.1435, and the recorder falls back to the model
card's CLS. **A significance test run on a broken benchmark measures the
breakage.** Both poolings ship so the comparison can be redone offline, and
gate 27 recomputes the recorded table from the shipped vectors rather than
trusting it.

### Dense retrieval does not resolve vocabulary mismatch

| | recall@10 on the vocabulary-mismatch slice |
|---|---:|
| BM25 | 0.033 |
| dense | 0.300 |

Nine times better, and it still misses 70%. PLAN §10a originally claimed dense
retrieval *resolves* vocabulary mismatch and gate 27's threshold was 0.50, set
from what "resolves" has to mean. The claim was withdrawn rather than the
threshold lowered — see PLAN §10a, amendment 2.

The evidence that this is the model's ceiling and not a corpus defect: strip
every word of filler and reduce the problem to choosing among **twelve**
candidate topics, and bge-small still picks the right one only **55.6%** of the
time. Two real defects were found and fixed on the way to establishing that,
and neither moved the number (0.13 → 0.33 → 0.30).

Chunking does not rescue it either. Chunk-level retrieval measured equal to or
worse than document-level across all four chunkings.

## Files

- `documents.jsonl` — `doc_id`, `type`, `text`, `facts`, `superseded`, `doc_index`.
- `queries.jsonl` — `query_id`, `text`, `gold_doc_ids` (empty for unanswerable),
  `phenomenon`, `note`.
- `../fixtures/embeddings/` — int8 vectors, per-vector scales, chunk metadata,
  and `manifest.json` recording the model, revision, licence, pooling decision
  and significance test.

## Open decision for Module 3

`documents.jsonl` is 4.2 MB and the embedding fixture is 11 MB. Both are fine in
git and fine for graders that run locally, but a lot to pull into a browser tab
for an in-page exercise. The likely resolution is a committed subset for Pyodide
exercises with the full corpus reserved for the capstone, but the subset size
should be chosen against a measured page load rather than guessed, so it is
deferred to the lesson that first needs it.
